Common multipoint door lock problems

Customers often describe what their door is doing rather than the name of the broken part. These symptoms have several possible causes; the correct repair starts with identifying the actual fault.

The UPVC door won't lock at the top or bottom

A hook, roller, shootbolt or other locking point may be binding, damaged or not reaching its keep. The door's alignment and mechanism both need checking.

The handle won't lift all the way

Locking points may be catching on keeps, the door may have dropped or the centre gearbox may be failing. Do not force the handle.

The hooks don't move when I lift the handle

A gearbox or connection within the locking strip may have failed. Different multipoint systems use different locking points and linkages.

The door only locks when I push or pull it

Pressure on the door can temporarily align locking points with the keeps. Hinge, sash and keep alignment may need attention.

The door locks when open but not when shut

This commonly suggests an alignment or keep problem, although worn hardware can contribute. Testing with the door open helps distinguish symptoms but does not confirm the cause.

The handle lifts but the key won't turn

The mechanism may not have fully engaged, or the cylinder or centre case may be faulty. Excessive force risks damage.

The door is jammed and won't open

A failed hook, bolt or centre case can leave locking points engaged. The door should be assessed carefully before attempting repair.

One locking point is stuck or scraping

A damaged hook, roller, shootbolt or keep can create resistance. The whole strip may not need replacement if a suitable repair is possible.

The multipoint lock is stiff or noisy

Wear, poor lubrication, corrosion or alignment issues can cause rough operation. The right repair depends on the condition of the mechanism.

The locking strip is broken or obsolete

Some mechanisms can be replaced with matching parts; others may require a suitable replacement system and careful compatibility checks.

Do I need a complete new multipoint locking mechanism?

Not always. Depending on the failure and parts availability, a centre gearbox, keep, handle or other component may be repairable or replaceable. A damaged or discontinued full strip may require a compatible replacement. The existing door and frame can often remain in place.

How are multipoint locks identified?

Manufacturer markings on the faceplate, backset, PZ measurement, faceplate width, lock length and positions of hooks, rollers or bolts can all matter. Clear photos of the door edge with the door safely open are useful. Never force a jammed mechanism just to take a photo.
