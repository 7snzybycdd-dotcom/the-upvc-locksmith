Common signs of a faulty UPVC door gearbox

Customers often describe what their door is doing rather than the name of the broken part. These symptoms have several possible causes; the correct repair starts with identifying the actual fault.

My UPVC door handle has gone floppy

A broken spring or worn component in the centre case can cause a drooping or loose handle. Handle furniture can also be responsible, so the gearbox should not be assumed faulty without checking.

The door handle won't lift

A seized centre case can stop the handle moving, but locking points binding against the frame keeps or a dropped door can produce similar symptoms.

The key turns but the door won't unlock

A cylinder may rotate without the mechanism releasing correctly if internal gearbox components have failed. A cylinder fault can cause similar behaviour.

The handle lifts but the key won't turn

The gearbox may not have reached its locking position, or the cylinder and mechanism may be under pressure. Check the cause rather than forcing the key.

The door only locks when it is open

If it works with the door open but not shut, alignment or keeps may be obstructing the locking points. Replacing the gearbox alone may not solve this.

The lock suddenly jammed shut

A broken gearbox component can leave the latch or locking points engaged. A jammed door needs careful diagnosis to avoid unnecessary damage.

The handle is stiff or crunches when operated

Wear or damage inside the gearbox is one possibility, but binding locking points and misalignment should also be investigated.

The handle moves but the hooks or bolts don't

A failed connection within the gearbox or multipoint strip may prevent movement reaching the locking points. The failed component needs identifying.

Can I replace just the centre gearbox?

Sometimes. If the multipoint strip is compatible and otherwise serviceable, a matching centre case may be replaced without changing the whole locking strip. Compatibility depends on details such as backset, PZ centres, spindle arrangement, case dimensions and how the gearbox connects to the strip. Older or discontinued locks may need a different repair solution.

What information helps identify the gearbox?

Photos of the door edge and faceplate markings, the handle arrangement and the lock with the door open can help. Do not dismantle a jammed or unsecured door to obtain photos. We can check the mechanism before advising on parts.

Matching the correct replacement

UPVC door mechanisms come in many designs and dimensions. Correct identification matters: measurements, faceplate details, spindle position, backset and the existing multipoint strip can all help determine the appropriate repair.
